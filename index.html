The Cover · Nº 8The tape, August 14

The crown goes to a ninth-round pick

Shem Hammon closes the season at .771, and the man he beat is the captain who drafted him in the ninth round. The margin is one tenth of a hit — thin enough that one more trip to the plate without a knock would have handed the whole thing back. He did not take that trip, or any other: on the final afternoon Hammon never batted, having begun it sixth. He finished the year first in the league and absent from the field, which is either the coolest thing anyone has done all season or the luckiest, and the book does not keep a column for the difference.

Methodology: stats and standings harvested from cpsoftball.com with the August 14, 2026 data — the final regular-season snapshot. The game book now carries all 120 games of the season, and every one of them reconciles exactly against the standings: each club's record, runs for and runs against match to the digit, which is the only reason this paper trusts harvested scores. Adjusted average = (H − CO) ÷ AB, verified exact on all 144 rows; the league line stands at .560, and a caused out erases a hit. The afternoon = games 19–20, played August 14 and joined on (team, draft pick) against the August 7 snapshot; a period's rate = (ΔH − ΔCO) ÷ ΔAB, and a line written 8-for-8 reads hits raw, rate net. The batting race takes all bats with 15 or more at-bats; hits back = (leader's average − the chaser's average) × the chaser's own at-bats, which is why a player with a hundred at-bats can sit a tenth of a hit behind a player with thirty-five. The finish chart plots that same figure on a zero-based axis — the tape at the leader, distance measured in hits behind — with every marker placed from the computed number rather than by eye, and the full figures repeated in the table beneath it.
