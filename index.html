The CoverGames 11 and 12

The crown changes hands

Cuervo Timpson caused his first out of the season and lost the batting title with it. The man who takes it wore it first.

He led this race for two straight editions, and he did not go quietly: fifteen-for-twenty-two that afternoon, heavy, honest work. But one of those at-bats erased a teammate — the first caused out of Cuervo Timpson's season — and the ledger is merciless about it. His line fell .864 to .788; his rank fell first to fourth, in one afternoon. The crown passes to Sefton Dockstader, at .816 — and it is a reclamation, not a coronation: Sefton led the very first edition of this paper, back on June 12, at .933. Curtis Knudson sits third. The analyst finds no conflict of interest and, once again, declines to recuse himself.

Hits above the book (HAE) = ΔH − ΔCO − ΔAB × own prior line — the Bat of the Afternoon statistic; the Anvil is the worst swing on 4+ afternoon at-bats; the Vacuum counts afternoon caused outs with ties broken by season total; the Ghost is the highest-ranked player with zero afternoon at-bats; the Iron Week is most afternoon at-bats; the Ladder and the Chute are the extreme league-rank moves. The Rebound Ledger's cohort = swing ≤ −.300 on 4+ at-bats in games 9–10; REBOUNDED = afternoon rate above the player's own July 10 line.
