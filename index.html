The LedgerStatement of account · Games 13 & 14

The accounting desk has the paper this week. Averages remain the league's adjusted average — (hits − caused outs) ÷ at-bats — and an afternoon is written as a line: the hits are raw, the rate is net, so a 7-for-8 afternoon with two caused outs posts .625. Every score below was reconciled against the standings to the run before a word of this was written. And because this week's cover is an audit, one more house number up front: the Pythagorean expectation is the winning percentage a club's runs scored and allowed would normally earn — PF² ÷ (PF² + PA²), a rule borrowed from baseball — and luck is how far a club's actual record sits above or below it. Readers hunting the deep season reference — draft board, round rooms, the full docket — will still find it filed in the July 10 archive.

The second opinion

Following the findings above, the desk ordered a medical review of the benchmark's condition and referred the case to the league's only known physician: Dr. Ben Williams of Boyds Daniel's team — bedside manner excellent, plate manner under review. The consult collapsed at intake, on grounds of conflict: the doctor is also a Williams, is himself presenting with a .250 week, and the nearest qualified specialist is his own father — Charlie Williams, born 1958, who took the field the same afternoon with no symptoms and posted the biggest workload in the league for the first-place club. Doctors make the worst patients. Fathers make the worst control groups. Physician, heal thy swing.
